EU Importers' Guide to Jute Bag Documentation

First container from India? Here's every document between a Kolkata factory and an EU warehouse — who issues it, when you need it, and where first-timers slip.

Azad Global Trade
21 October 2024
6 min read
EU Market

Your container can cross two oceans in five weeks and then sit at a European port for another two — all because one field on one form didn't match the goods.

This guide is the paperwork, in the order it actually happens, written for a first-time importer of jute bags from India. No customs-broker jargon. Just what each document is for, who issues it, and where we've watched people slip.

1. First: You Are the Importer of Record

The moment goods land in the UK or EU, customs looks at one entity: the importer of record. That's you — not the factory in West Bengal.

Suppliers can prepare documents, but the legal responsibility for the declaration, the duty and the VAT sits with the buyer, or with your customs broker acting for you. Everything else below follows from that one fact.

2. Your EORI Number — Sort It Today

An EORI number is free and takes about ten minutes to apply for online. UK numbers start with GB; EU member states issue their own country-prefixed codes. Without one, your goods cannot clear customs.

We've watched containers rack up storage charges at Felixstowe and Rotterdam because someone assumed the EORI could be sorted after shipping. Apply first. It costs nothing, and it's the one document only you can arrange.

3. The Four Documents Every Consignment Carries

Whatever the bag style, the paper stack looks the same. Here's what each one does:

  • Commercial invoice — the record of sale: buyer, seller, goods description, unit values, total, currency and Incoterm. Duty is calculated from this, so declare real values. Under-declaring is fraud, and customs authorities are reasonably good at spotting a "£0.10 jute bag".
  • Packing list — carton-by-carton contents and weights. It must match the invoice and the physical goods, exactly.
  • Certificate of origin — issued by the chamber of commerce at our end. For EU shipments, the EUR.1 certificate secures preferential duty under the EU–India arrangement; UK shipments may use GSP Form A where applicable. We flag it on the invoice when it applies.
  • Phytosanitary certificate — jute is a plant fibre, so shipments — particularly food-contact and bulk sacking lines — travel with this plant-health inspection certificate.

Every consignment we ship carries all four. The full list of licences and quality certificates we hold is on our documentation page.

4. HS Codes, Duty and VAT — the Honest Version

Jute sacks and bags used for packing goods generally classify under HS heading 6305.10. Decorative or structured handbags can sit elsewhere, which changes the duty rate — so the wording of your goods description matters more than most buyers expect. "Jute products" invites a customs query; "printed jute shopping bags, 320 GSM, laminated" doesn't.

We won't quote you a duty percentage in a blog post. Rates shift, and your exact ten-digit code decides what you pay — five minutes with your customs agent or the UK Global Tariff / EU TARIC tools tells you the number for your line. Then add VAT — 20% in the UK, with varying rates across EU member states — calculated on the customs value plus duty. Build your retail price on that landed cost, not the invoice value.

5. Who Handles What: FOB vs CIF vs DDP

The Incoterm on your quotation decides who books freight and who clears customs. For a first container, the difference is the whole experience:

CIF EU/UK portUsYour brokerBuyers who want predictable freight costs
DDP deliveredUsUs (duty included)First-time importers

DDP costs more per unit and removes the most headaches. Most first-time European buyers start there and move to CIF once they know their volumes — the full trade terms are on our export policy page.

6. Six Mistakes First-Time Importers Make

  1. Booking freight before the EORI exists. Goods arrive; paperwork can't.
  2. Vague invoice descriptions. "Jute products" gets queried. "Printed jute shopping bags, 320 GSM, laminated" clears.
  3. Under-declaring value. Penalties outweigh the saving every single time.
  4. Pricing retail off the invoice instead of the landed cost.
  5. Assuming the supplier can be the importer of record. It can't.
  6. Letting documents drift from the physical goods. Mismatches are the most common cause of clearance delays we see.

None of these are hard to avoid. All of them are expensive to discover. Send us your planned volume and destination — we'll put the document list and a landed-cost picture together before you commit to anything.

FAQ

Common questions about importing jute bags to the UK & EU

Do I need an import licence to bring jute bags into the UK or EU?
No specific licence is needed for jute bags in either market. You need an EORI number, a customs declaration (usually filed by your broker) and correct documents. The duty rate depends on the exact HS classification.
What is an EORI number and how do I get one?
An Economic Operators Registration and Identification number — free, applied for online, usually issued within days. UK numbers start with GB; EU member states issue their own country-prefixed codes. Apply before your goods ship, not after.
Who pays import duty if I buy DDP?
Under DDP (Delivered Duty Paid), we arrange freight, clearance and duty, and the quoted price covers all of it. Your only task is receiving the goods.
How long does EU/UK customs clearance take?
With clean, matching documents, typically 1–3 days. The most common cause of delay is a mismatch between the invoice, packing list and physical goods — which is why we check all three against each other before dispatch.
Can Azad Global Trade ship to my customs broker?
Yes. Give us your broker's details and they handle clearance; the goods move to them on arrival. Common for buyers without their own bonded storage.
